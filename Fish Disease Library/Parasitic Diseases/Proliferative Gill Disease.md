## Overview

### What is Proliferative Gill Disease (PGD)?
Proliferative Gill Disease (PGD) is an emerging problem in salmonid aquaculture, particularly affecting Atlantic salmon (*Salmo salar*) in both freshwater and marine environments [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426) [4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00705-023-05866-8). It is characterized by proliferative changes in gill tissue and can be associated with various pathogens, including parasites, bacteria, and viruses [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426) [4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00705-023-05866-8). PGD leads to significant economic losses due to high mortality rates, impaired respiratory function, and reduced growth performance in farmed salmon [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).

## Clinical Signs of Proliferative Gill Disease

### Common Signs

- **Physical Signs:**
    - **Gill Proliferation:** Thickened, swollen, and pale gills with excessive mucus production [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426) [2](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms9122605).
    - **Gill Lesions:** White or greyish nodules and lesions on the gills [2](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms9122605).
    - **Haemorrhages:** Reddened areas in the gill tissues due to bleeding [2](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms9122605).
    - **Respiratory Distress:** Rapid or labored breathing due to impaired gill function [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).

- **Behavioural Changes:**
    - **Lethargy:** Reduced activity and slow swimming [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).
    - **Loss of Appetite:** Decreased feeding behaviour and significant weight loss [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).
    - **Abnormal Swimming Patterns:** Fish may swim near the surface or close to aeration points to compensate for reduced oxygen uptake [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).

**Progression of Clinical Signs**

- **Early Stages:** Mild signs such as reduced feed intake and slight lethargy [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).

- **Advanced Stages:** Pronounced physical signs including gill proliferation, lesions, and respiratory distress, leading to high mortality if not managed [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426) [2](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms9122605).

- **Impact on Fish Health:** PGD severely compromises respiratory function, leading to stress, reduced growth rates, and higher susceptibility to secondary infections [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).

### Causes of Proliferative Gill Disease

**Etiology**

- **Causative Agents:** Multiple pathogens can be associated with PGD in salmonids, including:
    - **Parasites:** *Ichthyobodo* spp., *Neoparamoeba* sp. [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426) [4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00705-023-05866-8)
    - **Bacteria:** *Flexibacter*/*Flavobacterium*, '*Candidatus* Branchiomonas cysticola' [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426) [4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00705-023-05866-8)
    - **Viruses:** Salmon gill poxvirus (SGPV) [4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00705-023-05866-8)

- **Transmission Methods:**
    - **Waterborne:** Pathogens spread through water, particularly in conditions of high fish density and poor water quality [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).
    - **Direct Contact:** Spread between infected and healthy fish through close proximity [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).

**Risk Factors**

- **Environmental Factors:** Poor water quality, high stocking densities, and stress conditions can increase susceptibility to PGD [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).

- **Farm Management Practices:** Ineffective biosecurity measures and lack of routine health monitoring can facilitate the spread of pathogens [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).

### Diagnosis

**Diagnostic Methods**

- **Clinical Examination:** Observation of physical and behavioural signs indicative of PGD [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426) [2](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms9122605).

- **Laboratory Tests:**
    - **Histopathology:** Microscopic examination of gill tissues to identify characteristic lesions and pathogens [2](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms9122605).
    - **PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction):** Detects specific pathogens associated with PGD, such as SGPV [4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00705-023-05866-8).
    - **Gross Pathological Scoring:** A novel total gill scoring system has been developed to assess gross pathological changes [2](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms9122605).

**Differential Diagnosis**

- **Distinguishing PGD from Other Gill Diseases:** It is crucial to differentiate PGD from other gill diseases, such as amoebic gill disease (AGD) and complex gill disease (CGD) [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426) [2](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms9122605) [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).

### Treatment and Prevention

**Treatment Options**

- **Current Treatments:** There are no specific treatments for PGD. Management focuses on preventing the spread and mitigating the impact through biosecurity measures and supportive care [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).
- **Experimental Treatments:** Research is ongoing into potential antiparasitic agents and other therapeutic approaches [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).

**Preventive Measures**

- **Biosecurity Protocols:**
    - Implementing strict quarantine measures for new stock to prevent the introduction of pathogens [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).
    - Regular disinfection of equipment and facilities to minimize the risk of contamination [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).

- **Farm Management Practices:**
    - Reducing stocking densities to decrease the risk of pathogen transmission [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).
    - Regular health monitoring and early detection programs to identify and manage outbreaks promptly [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426) [2](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms9122605) [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).
    - Implementing all-in/all-out stocking practices to reduce the risk of spreading infections [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).

### Case Studies

**Real-World Examples**

- **Notable Outbreaks:**
    - **Norway (2006):** Discovery of salmon gill poxvirus (SGPV) in Atlantic salmon suffering from PGD in freshwater and marine sites [4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00705-023-05866-8).
    - **Norway and Ireland:** Identification of '*Candidatus* Branchiomonas cysticola' as a common agent of epitheliocysts in seawater-farmed Atlantic salmon [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).
    - **Global:** Increasing reports of complex gill disease (CGD) in Atlantic salmon aquaculture [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).

- **Response Strategies and Outcomes:**
    - **Norway:** Development of genotyping tools for SGPV to better understand its epidemiology and spread [4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00705-023-05866-8).
    - **Chile:** Implementation of improved diagnostic methods, including gross pathological scoring systems [2](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms9122605).
    - **Industry-wide:** Increased focus on multifactorial approaches to understanding and managing gill diseases in salmon aquaculture [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).

- **Lessons Learned:** The importance of considering multiple pathogens and environmental factors in the etiology and management of PGD in salmonids [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).

## Data Insights
### Disease Impact by Country
#### Norway

- **PGD Incidence in Norway:**
    - Proliferative gill disease (PGD) is an emerging problem in Norwegian culture of Atlantic salmon (*Salmo salar*) [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426) [4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00705-023-05866-8).
    - A new poxvirus, salmon gill poxvirus (SGPV), was identified in 2006 on the gills of salmon from a gill disease outbreak that began in November 2005 at a freshwater smolt production site in northern Norway [4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00705-023-05866-8).

- **Geographical Spread:**
    - SGPV was found on gills of salmon at two marine sites in western Norway later in 2006 [4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00705-023-05866-8).
    - PGD is particularly common in farmed Atlantic salmon in Norway [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).

- **Economic Impact:**
    - Mortality reached about 20% at the first affected freshwater site and close to 90% at a marine site in western Norway [4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00705-023-05866-8).
    - PGD can lead to elevated mortality rates, reduced growth rates, and reduced food conversion in salmon [1](https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12426).

- **Treatment & Management:**
    - Management focuses on identifying and monitoring potential pathogens associated with gill disease [3](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0222926).

## Conclusion

Proliferative Gill Disease (PGD) remains a significant challenge for aquaculture, necessitating diligent management and preventive measures. By implementing robust biosecurity protocols, investing in ongoing research, and fostering industry-wide cooperation, fish health managers and veterinarians can mitigate the impact of PGD and ensure the sustainability of aquaculture operations. This comprehensive guide aims to provide the necessary information and resources to effectively manage PGD and support the health and welfare of farmed fish.
