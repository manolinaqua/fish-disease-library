## Overview

### What is Paranucleospora theridion?

*Paranucleospora theridion* (also called *Desmozoon lepeophtherii)* is a microsporidian that infects several cell types in salmon and salmon lice. It is one of several agents found in gill disease, including proliferative gill inflammation (PGI) and "autumn sickness" (høstsyken) [1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s44375-026-00008-2) [2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/raq.12426)[3](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/kronisk-gjellebetennelse-hos-laks) It was first found in sea lice in Scotland in 2000  [2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/raq.12426) and was described in farmed Atlantic salmon with gill disease in western Norway in 2008 [4](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02313). It is also reported from Scotland, Ireland and the Pacific coast of North America, including wild Chinook and coho in British Columbia, where no disease was described [2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/raq.12426), [5](https://www.int-res.com/journals/meps/articles/meps14581). In Norway it threatens farmed salmon and wild trout, with levels highest in the south [1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s44375-026-00008-2)

Infection is common and does not always mean disease, but numbers are high in cases such as PGI [4](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02313) [2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/raq.12426). It is not established how much the parasite causes the damage [2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/raq.12426). In one Norwegian outbreak in 2008 it appeared to act alone, causing an acute systemic infection that later became chronic, with 5.6–12.8% mortality and stunted growth [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5). Why some infections become systemic is unknown, though temperature may play a part [7](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02464).

It needs both a louse and a fish to complete its life cycle  [1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s44375-026-00008-2). Spores in the water probably infect salmon, but how the parasite gets from lice back to fish is not known [2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/raq.12426), [7](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02464). It is easily confused with other causes of gill disease, because several agents are involved and it is unclear which are primary [4](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02313). No known treatment available.

## Clinical Signs of Paranucleospora theridion

### Common Signs

- **Physical Signs:**
    - **Pale and Slimy Gills:** Gills are pale, patchy and slimy with a lot of mucus [4](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02313) [3](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/kronisk-gjellebetennelse-hos-laks)
	    - Under microscope; gill lining is thickened and swollen [4](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02313)
	- **Skin bleeding** at the fin bases or scale loss [4](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02313), [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5)
    - **Inflammation of organs:** in the kidney, muscle and heart [4](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02313)
	- **Melanomacrophages;** Many in the kidney and spleen [4](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02313)
	- **In the 2008 acute outbreak:** *Note: It was not established if P. theridion was the causative pathogen, but no other pathogen was found*
		- **Dark greenish skin**: in the acute outbreak [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5)
		- **Swollen spleen**, fluid in the abdomen and inflammation of the abdominal lining (peritonitis) [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5)
		- **Inflammation cutting deep into the gut wall**, with stunted growth and unexpected vaccine reactions. The authors suggest checking for the parasite [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5)

- **Behavioural Changes:**
    - **Lethargy:** Reduced activity [3](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/kronisk-gjellebetennelse-hos-laks)though fled when netted, and had stopped eating [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5)
    - **Increased jumping:** In one farm in September, fish jumped much more and showed skin irritation with extra mucus. No cause was found, but skin samples from 7 fish had high parasite numbers  [7](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02464). The authors say spore exposure may possibly be responsible  [7](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02464).
    - For [[Proliferative Gill Disease|Proliferative Gill Inflammation]], fish can have open mouth, distended gill covers and fish 'standing' against the current near the net edge, trying to maximise oxygen exchange [9](https://akvademiet.no/fiskesykdommer/infeksjonssykdommer/pgi-proliferativ-gjellebetennelse/)


**Progression of Symptoms**

- **Acute stage:** Widespread cell death in gills and abdomen, with the parasite spread through most organs [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5)
- **Chronic stage:** Thick gill lining and ongoing inflammation, with few parasites [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5)
- **Early Stages:** Reduced feeding, pale slimy gills  [7](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02464). Early signs seems to come from the parasite, and later damage may be the fish's own response [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5)

- **Advanced Stages:** Can develop to systemic infection with infection of cell types such as gill and skin epithelial cells, blood vessel endothelial cells, polymorphonuclear leucocytes and macrophage-like cells and organ inflammation [4](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02313)[6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5)

- **Impact on Fish Health:** In the acute outbreak mortality totalled 5.6–12.8% and sick fish weighed 20–25% of normal fish [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5)

### Causes of Paranucleosporosis

**Etiology**
- **Life cycle:** 
	- The parasite needs both salmon and lice. In salmon it goes through two development cycles, and each makes a different spore [7](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02464), [8](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1550-7408.2009.00451.x).
	- Cycle I happens inside white blood cells and skin cells. It makes many small, thin-walled spores  [7](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02464), [8](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1550-7408.2009.00451.x)
	- These spores are thought to infect more cells within the same fish, which spreads the infection through the body  [7](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02464), [8](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1550-7408.2009.00451.x)
	- Cycle II happens in the nuclei of skin and gill cells. It makes a few thick-walled spores  [7](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02464)
	- These tougher spores are thought to pass the parasite to lice (*Lepeophtheirus salmonis*), which ingest them when feeding on salmon skin  [7](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02464)
	- In lice the parasite goes through a third cycle, and a heavily infected lice can be packed with spores  [7](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02464)

- **Transmission Methods:**
    - **Waterborne:** Salmon are most likely infected by spores in the water, but how the parasite gets from lice back to salmon is not fully known [2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/raq.12426), [7](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02464). Though it is suspected that dying lice, full of spores in the winter is likely responsible for a resvoir of spores in the water come spring  [7](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02464). Healthy fish can also carry the parasite [2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/raq.12426). 
    

**Risk Factors**

**Environmental**
- **Temperature:** it multiplied in salmon at 14.5–17.1 °C and in lice at 10.5–15.2 °C  [7](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02464). The 2011 authors expect disease mainly where water reaches 15 °C in summer and autumn [4](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02313).
- **Season:** Show clear seasonal pattern, peaking in autumn in farmed fish [10](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0044848616305117) and in summer in wild fish  [5](https://www.int-res.com/journals/meps/articles/meps14581).
- **Salinity:** in August, infection was higher on the outer coast (23.5 ppt) than in the Hardangerfjord (16.1 ppt). This is one comparison [10](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0044848616305117)

**Host Specific** 
- Highest density of *P. theridion* in fish with poor condition [10](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0044848616305117)

**Farm Management**
- **Handling:** severe gill disease typically follows handling or lice treatment [11](https://www.gov.scot/binaries/content/documents/govscot/publications/foi-eir-release/2018/03-a/foi-18-00469/documents/foi-18-00469-ghi-abstracts-final-gill-health-initiative-2017-pdf/foi-18-00469-ghi-abstracts-final-gill-health-initiative-2017-pdf/govscot%3Adocument/FOI-18-00469%2B-%2BGHI%2BAbstracts%2BFinal%2B-%2BGill%2BHealth%2BInitiative%2B2017.pdf).

### Diagnosis

**Diagnostic Methods**

- **Clinical Examination:** A gill smear under the microscope can rule out look-alikes such as _Ichthyobodo_, _Trichodina_ and _Neoparamoeba_ [4](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02313)

- **Laboratory Tests:**
    - **Positive result may not mean disease causing:** *P. theridion* can be found in healthy fish, so a positive result alone does not show if it will have the disease [2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/raq.12426)
	    - For example, *P. theridion* was found in 100% of samples from 13 farms [10](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0044848616305117).
	    - However, higher numbers point more towards disease. Fish with proliferative gill disease had significantly higher levels of *P. theridion* than others [4](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02313)
    - **PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction):** PCR is the most sensitive and specific way to show the parasite is present [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5)
    - **Histopathology:** Co-infections make histological diagnosis of gill disease difficult  [2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/raq.12426). Common stains significantly underestimated spore numbers and  calcofluor white or IHC is recommended [12](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/1040638719887707)


**Differential Diagnosis**

- Agents linked to gill disease include _Ichthyobodo_, _Neoparamoeba perurans_, epitheliocyst bacteria, Atlantic salmon paramyxovirus and salmon gill poxvirus [4](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02313)
- The parasite is also found in fish with pancreas disease, [[Heart and Muscle Inflammation (HSMI)]]and [[Cardiomyopathy Syndrome (CMS)]] [4](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02313). Its numbers showed no link to SAV or [[Pancreas Disease (PD)]] outbreaks [10](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0044848616305117).
- _Tenacibaculum_ often colonises tissue already damaged by this parasite [2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/raq.12426)

### Treatment and Prevention

**Treatment Options**

- **Current Treatments:** 
	- No current treatment available for *P. theridion*.

- **Prevention:**
	- No prevention methods were found
	- **Biosecurity Protocols:**
	    - Fallowing: No source tests fallowing against _P. theridion_. In two farms fallowed for about nine months to a year, the next generation still had high to extreme levels [4](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02313). The source of that infection is not stated [4](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02313)

- **Farm Management and Supportive Care**
	- For gill disease in general, avoid moving, handling or stressing affected fish, and investigate bleeding gills [3](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/kronisk-gjellebetennelse-hos-laks)
	- Measures used for complex gill disease include extra oxygen, freshwater baths, skirts against algae and jellyfish, functional feeds [2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/raq.12426)
	
- **Experimental Treatments:** 
	- The parasite has not been grown in the lab, so controlled trials are difficult [3](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/kronisk-gjellebetennelse-hos-laks).


### Case Studies

**Real-World Examples*

- **Published paper:** "A case study of _Desmozoon lepeophtherii_ infection in farmed Atlantic salmon associated with gill disease, peritonitis, intestinal infection, stunted growth, and increased mortality" Authors: Simon Chioma Weli, Ole Bendik Dale, Haakon Hansen, Mona Cecilie Gjessing, Liv Birte Rønneberg & Knut Falk, 2017. [Link to Study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5)
  
	- **Where and when:** a seawater farm in a fjord on the west coast of north-western Norway. Atlantic salmon smolts stocked in spring 2008.
	  
	- **How was it discovered:** an acute disease problem began in August 2008, in fish averaging about 400 g. Fish were sampled in September 2008, November 2008 and January 2009. The parasite was confirmed with real-time PCR, DNA sequencing and in situ hybridisation. No other known salmon disease was found, including IPN.
	  
	- **What the fish looked like;** Affected fish swam sluggishly but fled when netted, had stopped eating, and often had dark, greenish skin [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5). Gills were uneven with extra mucus, and some fish had small skin bleedings at the fin bases [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5). Findings inside the abdomen were variable liver colour, a swollen spleen, fluid, congestion and often peritonitis [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5). A few fish had a grey, cheese-like, enlarged kidney [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5).
	  
	- **Handling:** the paper reports no treatment or management actions. It is a diagnostic study of a natural outbreak [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5)
	  
	- **Mortality:** daily deaths varied widely. Cumulative mortality from sea transfer to the end of January 2009 was 5.6–12.8% across eight net-pens [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5)
	  
	- **Growth:** sick fish weighed 20–25% of normal fish by the end of January 2009 [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5)
	  
	- **Cost:** the authors call the loss significant, mainly because of stunted growth and less because of mortality. No cost figure is given [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5)
	
	- **Source of infection:** sea lice were present but not causing problems, and the authors regard them as the source . The reason the infection built up is not known [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5)

## Data Insights
### Disease Impact by Country

#### Norway

- **Western Norway, 2008–09:** fish stocked in spring 2008 and averaging about 400 g became ill from August. Mortality was 5.6-12.8% and no other known disease was found [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-017-2303-5) *(see above in case studies for more details)*.
- **55-farm study:** levels were high in southern Norway and highest in gill disease farms [4](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02313). In one farm the first generation lost 80% and the next lost 14% after fallowing [4](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02313)
- **13 farms in western Norway:** SAV was found in six and five had pancreas disease. Parasite numbers did not predict which [10](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0044848616305117).
- **Two farms :** fish put to sea in May were infected within two months. Fish put to sea in November showed deaths put down to IPN, not this parasite  [7](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao02464).

#### Ireland

- On one farm, the parasite was in 80–100% of fish once established, from week 16 after sea transfer [13](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jfd.12814).
- In 2016 it was found in fish with gill problems near harvest, and its role was unclear [14](https://www.gov.scot/binaries/content/documents/govscot/publications/foi-eir-release/2018/03-a/foi-18-00469/documents/foi-18-00469-related-documents-handwritten-notes-part-1-pdf/foi-18-00469-related-documents-handwritten-notes-part-1-pdf/govscot%3Adocument/FOI-18-00469%2B-%2Brelated%2Bdocuments%2Bwith%2Bhandwritten%2Bnotes%2B-%2Bpart%2B1.pdf)

#### Scotland

- It is reported, with no case details in the sources [2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/raq.12426)

#### Canada (British Columbia)

- It was found in 65.1% of wild juvenile Chinook and 75.4% of coho tested, with no disease described  [5](https://www.int-res.com/journals/meps/articles/meps14581).

## Conclusion

_P. theridion_ is a common parasite of farmed salmon in Norway and is found in healthy fish as well as sick ones. High levels are linked to gill disease, and one outbreak with no other known disease was linked directly to severe damage and stunted growth. No treatment is described, so management rests on avoiding stress and handling in affected fish and reading a positive test alongside the fish's signs and pathology. Research is still working out whether the parasite causes the damage, how it spreads, and what it does to the immune system. Warming water may increase infections in northern farms and wild fish, and the parasite's inability to be grown in the lab makes controlled trials hard.
