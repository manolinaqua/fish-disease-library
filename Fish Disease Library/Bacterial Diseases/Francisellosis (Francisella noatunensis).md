## Overview

### What is Francisellosis (*Francisella noatunensis*)?

<!-- TODO fact-check: "1980s" and the "UA2" name are unverified. Formal reports of Francisella in fish date from the 1990s (tilapia, Taiwan) and 2000s (cod, Norway; Atlantic salmon, Chile). Check the source before keeping the UA2 alias. -->
In the 1980s, a pathogen with rickettsial characteristics was detected that was nonetheless different from *Piscirickettsia salmonis*. It was initially named "UA2" (unknown agent 2) to distinguish it from *P. salmonis*.

Francisellosis in farmed Atlantic salmon (*Salmo salar*) is caused by *Francisella noatunensis*. A related species, *Francisella orientalis*, causes francisellosis in tilapia. <!-- TODO cite -->

## Clinical Signs of Francisellosis (*Francisella noatunensis*)

### Common Signs

- **Physical Signs:**
    - Affected Atlantic salmon may show no external clinical signs [1](https://marcosgodoy.com/franciselosis-en-salmon-del-atlantico-salmo-salar-patologia-macroscopica/).
    - Internally, fish show hepatomegaly, renomegaly, and splenomegaly, with a variable presence of white nodules in a multifocal to coalescing distribution. Haemorrhages in the swim bladder are occasionally seen [1](https://marcosgodoy.com/franciselosis-en-salmon-del-atlantico-salmo-salar-patologia-macroscopica/).

<!-- TODO fact-check: everything from here to the END marker matches the classic ISA description (cf. [[Infectious Salmon Anemia (ISA)]], haematocrit below 10%) and contradicts ref [1], which reports no external signs. Verify with a Francisella-specific source or remove. -->
- **External Signs:**
    - Pale gills (possibly due to anaemia). In some cases blood accumulates in the gills, especially in the central venous sinus of the gill filaments.
    - Exophthalmia.
    - Distended abdomen.
    - Petechiae in the anterior eye chamber.
    - Haemorrhage in the abdomen.
    - Oedema of the scale pockets.
- **Internal Signs:**
    - The liver appears darkened due to haemorrhagic necrosis.
    - The kidney shows moderate inflammation with interstitial haemorrhage and tubular necrosis.
    - The intestine appears dark red, due to haemorrhage within the intestinal wall but not in the lumen.
- **Blood Parameters:**
    - Haematocrit below 10 in the final stages (between 25 and 30 in less severe cases).
    - Blood smears show vacuolated or degenerated erythrocytes and erythroblasts with irregularly shaped nuclei. The leukocyte-to-erythrocyte ratio is reduced, with the largest reductions in lymphocytes and thrombocytes.

### Causes of Francisellosis (*Francisella noatunensis*)

**Etiology**

- **Causative Agent:** *Francisella* sp. is a Gram-negative, non-motile, pleomorphic bacterium, ranging from coccoid forms to small rods. It is strictly aerobic and facultatively intracellular, measuring about 1.45 × 0.35 µm within the cytoplasmic vacuoles of phagocytes. <!-- TODO cite -->
- **Growth Requirements:** It is a fastidious microorganism that generally requires enriched culture media (5% blood, supplemented with cysteine). <!-- TODO cite -->
- **Transmission Methods:**
    - **Horizontal Transmission:** Transmission is mainly horizontal. <!-- TODO cite -->
