## Overview

### What is Salmonid Rickettsial Septicaemia (SRS)?

Salmonid Rickettsial Septicaemia (SRS), also known as Piscirickettsiosis, is a severe bacterial disease primarily affecting salmonids, including Atlantic salmon (*Salmo salar*), coho salmon (*Oncorhynchus kisutch*), and rainbow trout (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*). It has also been identified in lumpfish (*Cyclopterus lumpus*), European seabass (*Dicentrarchus labrax*), and turbot (*Scophthalmus maximus*) [30](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/piscirickettsiose). It is caused by *Piscirickettsia salmonis*, a facultative intracellular gram-negative bacterium that was the first "rickettsia-like" pathogen identified in fish [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.1019404) [17](https://marcosgodoy.com/piscirickettsia-salmonis-patogeno-intracelular-facultativo-observaciones-en-medios-solidos/).

First identified in Chile in 1989 during a devastating outbreak in coho salmon, SRS has since become the most economically significant disease in Chilean salmon aquaculture. The 1989 outbreak killed approximately 1.5 million coho salmon, causing economic losses of $10 million USD. By 1990, the disease had spread to Atlantic salmon with mortality rates reaching 90% on some farms, and by 1995, annual losses had risen to $49 million USD [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia) [3](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao035107).

Today, SRS is responsible for over $700 million USD in annual losses in Chile and accounts for 52.8% of all infectious disease mortality in Atlantic salmon (2022). The disease has also been reported in Norway, Canada, Scotland, and Ireland, though with considerably lower incidence and economic impact than in Chile [4](https://doi.org/10.1128/mbio.03821-24) [5](https://www.aquafeed.com/newsroom/reports/chile-publishes-annual-reports-on-aquaculture-health-status-and-antimicrobial-use/).

## Clinical Signs of Salmonid Rickettsial Septicaemia (SRS)

### Common Signs

- **Physical Signs:**
    - **Darkened Coloration:** Affected fish exhibit an overall darkening of the skin [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia) [15](https://marcosgodoy.com/presentacion-cutanea-de-la-piscirickettsiosis-piscirickettsia-salmonis-en-salmon-coho-oncorhynchus-kisutch-patologia-macroscopica/).
    - **Anaemia and Pale Gills:** Severe anaemia leading to pale gills is a hallmark sign [6](https://aquatechcenter.com/disease_challenge/salmon-rickettsial-syndrome-srs/).
    - **Haemorrhages and Skin Lesions:** Petechial haemorrhages, vesicles with scale elevations, and skin ulcers with underlying muscle haemorrhages (Figure 1)[15](https://marcosgodoy.com/presentacion-cutanea-de-la-piscirickettsiosis-piscirickettsia-salmonis-en-salmon-coho-oncorhynchus-kisutch-patologia-macroscopica/).
    - **Swollen Organs:** Enlarged kidneys and spleen upon internal examination [16](https://marcosgodoy.com/es-septicemia-rickettsial-salmonidea-srs-en-salmon-del-atlantico-salmo-salar-iv-presentacion-clinica-patologica-visceral/).
    - **Liver Lesions:** Ring-shaped, cream-colored, and occasionally haemorrhagic liver lesions in chronic cases. Affected livers are often pale, mottled, and in some cases may appear yellowish [16](https://marcosgodoy.com/es-septicemia-rickettsial-salmonidea-srs-en-salmon-del-atlantico-salmo-salar-iv-presentacion-clinica-patologica-visceral/) [30](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/piscirickettsiose).
    - **Scale Loss and Fin Fraying:** External degradation of scales and fins [15](https://marcosgodoy.com/presentacion-cutanea-de-la-piscirickettsiosis-piscirickettsia-salmonis-en-salmon-coho-oncorhynchus-kisutch-patologia-macroscopica/).
    - **Fluid in abdomen (ascites):** Accumulation of fluid in the abdominal cavity may cause visible abdominal swelling in severely affected fish [30](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/piscirickettsiose). 
    - **Internal Haemorrhages:** Haemorrhages may occur in internal organs, particularly in the liver, intestine, pyloric caeca, peritoneal fat, and swim bladder, reflecting the systemic septicaemic nature of the disease [30](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/piscirickettsiose). 

- **Behavioural Signs:**
    - **Lethargy:** Reduced activity and slow swimming [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia).
    - **Loss of Appetite:** Decreased feeding behaviour and significant weight loss [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia).
    - **Respiratory Distress:** Increased gill movement and labored breathing[1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia).
    - **Surface Swimming:** Fish congregating near the water surface [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia).

**Progression of Clinical Signs**

- **Early Stages:** Subtle signs such as reduced feed intake and slight lethargy. The disease can be insidious with initially low mortality. SRS mortality risk has a strong seasonal component, with mortalities significantly higher in warmer seasons [26](https://doi.org/10.1111/jfd.70097) [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia).
- **Advanced Stages:** Pronounced physical signs including severe anaemia, internal haemorrhages, organ enlargement, and characteristic liver lesions. In acute cases, death may occur with few or no obvious gross pathological signs. Acute outbreaks can cause mortality rates of 30-90%. Experimental co-infection with the LF-89 and EM-90 strains resulted in only 24.1% survival after 21 days, compared with 40.3% survival in fish infected with EM-90 alone [25](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13567-024-01356-0) [4](https://doi.org/10.1128/mbio.03821-24) [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia).

- **Impact on Fish Health:** SRS severely compromises immune function, physiological condition and overall vitality. Mortality can vary considerably depending on geographic region, fish condition, and the presence of additional stressors or co-infections. Average cumulative mortality during an 18-month saltwater production cycle is approximately 20% in Chile, while in Norway losses of approximately 5–6% within individual sea cages have been reported at most [25](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13567-024-01356-0) [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia)[30](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/piscirickettsiose).

### Causes of Salmonid Rickettsial Septicaemia (SRS)

**Etiology**

- **Causative Agent:** *Piscirickettsia salmonis*, a facultative intracellular gram-negative bacterium, typically 0.5-1.5 μm in diameter. Originally described as obligate intracellular, it is now recognized as a facultative intracellular pathogen capable of growth in cell-free media. It was the first "rickettsia-like" bacterium to be recognized as a pathogen of fish [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.1019404) [17](https://marcosgodoy.com/piscirickettsia-salmonis-patogeno-intracelular-facultativo-observaciones-en-medios-solidos/). Genotypic variation within *Piscirickettsia salmonis* has been reported across different geographic regions, including Chile, Scotland, and Ireland, and mixed infections involving multiple strains may occur. Evidence suggests that different variants differ in virulence, disease severity, and associated mortality [30](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/piscirickettsiose). 

- **Transmission Methods:** The natural reservoir of *Piscirickettsia salmonis* remains unclear [30](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/piscirickettsiose).
    - **Horizontal Transmission:** Direct water-borne transmission; the bacterium can survive several weeks at 5-20°C in saltwater. Experimentally, entry occurs through intact skin and gills [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia). Transmission is enhanced under high stocking densities in experimental studies [30](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/piscirickettsiose).
    - **Vertical Transmission:** Possible transmission through eggs via an adhesion complex that allows pathogen entry [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia).
    - **Vector Transmission:** In Chile, parasitic organisms such as the isopod *Ceratothoa gaudichaudii* and sea lice of the genus *Caligus* (see [[Caligidosis (Caligus rogercresseyi)]]) have been associated with enhanced transmission and worsened disease outcomes [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.1019404) [30](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/piscirickettsiose).

**Risk Factors**

- **Environmental Factors:** Water temperatures between 9-16°C and a salinity > 26 PSU increase disease risk [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.1019404). Low oxygen conditions and harmful algal blooms predispose fish to infection [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.1019404) [5](https://www.aquafeed.com/newsroom/reports/chile-publishes-annual-reports-on-aquaculture-health-status-and-antimicrobial-use/). Sea lice co-infections are considered important risk factors that may reduce the effectiveness of vaccination under farm conditions [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.1019404).
- **Farm Management Practices:** High stocking densities, overcrowding, chemical delousing treatments, and net fouling that reduces oxygen availability and cause stress all increase susceptibility [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.1019404).

### Diagnosis

**Diagnostic Methods**

- **Clinical Examination:** Gross lesion examination with observation of characteristic darkened coloration, swollen organs, and ring-shaped liver lesions. Hepatic nodules (moderate and severe) are strongly associated with EM-90-like infection specifically [15](https://marcosgodoy.com/presentacion-cutanea-de-la-piscirickettsiosis-piscirickettsia-salmonis-en-salmon-coho-oncorhynchus-kisutch-patologia-macroscopica/) [16](https://marcosgodoy.com/es-septicemia-rickettsial-salmonidea-srs-en-salmon-del-atlantico-salmo-salar-iv-presentacion-clinica-patologica-visceral/) [19](https://doi.org/10.3390/pathogens12030450). Confirmation of the diagnosis is achieved through isolation of the pathogen and/or serological detection of the causative organism [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia).

**Genogroup Identification**

- **Importance of Genogroup Differentiation:** LF-89-like became the most prevalent genogroup in Chilean salmon aquaculture between 2017-2021, while EM-90-like is associated with specific hepatic lesions. Current EM-90-based vaccines show incomplete cross-protection against LF-89, making genogroup identification critical for vaccination strategy [19](https://doi.org/10.3390/pathogens12030450) [20](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2021.673216).

**Differential Diagnosis**

- **Distinguishing SRS from Other Diseases:** It is crucial to differentiate SRS from other conditions with similar clinical signs, including [[Infectious Salmon Anemia (ISA)]], which causes similar haemorrhaging, [[Bacterial Kidney Disease (Renibacterium salmoninarum)]], which also involves kidney pathology, [[Furunculosis (Aeromonas spp.)|furunculosis]], and [[Tenacibaculosis (Tenacibaculum spp)|tenacibaculosis]], which generates similar skin lesions [6](https://aquatechcenter.com/disease_challenge/salmon-rickettsial-syndrome-srs/).

- **Chilean Regulatory Requirement (Sernapesca Resolution 1606/2021):** In Chile, PCR-based discrimination between SRS and Tenacibaculosis is mandatory. The updated Tenacibaculosis definition requires *Tenacibaculum* spp. identification by PCR, with external signs (fin erosion, oral/rostral lesions with yellow pigmentation, gill ulcers with yellowish coloration), **and explicitly excludes cases showing evident lesions of Piscirickettsiosis or Renibacteriosis**. This regulatory requirement addresses the high co-occurrence of these pathogens, ensuring proper epidemiological classification [22](https://www.sernapesca.cl/noticias/sernapesca-actualiza-definicion-de-enfermedad-tenacibaculosis/).

- **Differentiating Clinical Signs:**
    - **Piscirickettsiosis:** Primarily a systemic disease characterized by ring-shaped liver lesions (cream/yellow), internal granulomatous lesions in viscera, kidney and spleen enlargement. Skin ulcers are secondary to systemic infection [15](https://marcosgodoy.com/presentacion-cutanea-de-la-piscirickettsiosis-piscirickettsia-salmonis-en-salmon-coho-oncorhynchus-kisutch-patologia-macroscopica/) [16](https://marcosgodoy.com/es-septicemia-rickettsial-salmonidea-srs-en-salmon-del-atlantico-salmo-salar-iv-presentacion-clinica-patologica-visceral/).
    - **Tenacibaculosis:** Primarily an external bacterial disease characterized by external ulcerative lesions with yellow pigmentation, mouth erosion, fin necrosis, tail rot. Body and caudal fin lesions (*T. maritimum*) or head/snout lesions (*T. dicentrarchi*). **Unlike SRS, tenacibaculosis does not typically produce characteristic systemic or internal lesions** [23](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcimb.2022.1068000).

**Bacterial Consortia in Skin Ulcers**

- **Core Pathobiome:** Research has identified a complex polymicrobial community referred to as a core pathobiome, in Atlantic salmon skin ulcers during *P. salmonis* infection. The primary pathobiome consists of *P. salmonis*, *Tenacibaculum dicentrarchi*, and *Aliivibrio wodanis*, with *Vibrio* spp. enriched on infected gills. Four bacterial families are overrepresented in ulcerated tissue: Vibrionaceae, Flavobacteriaceae, Piscirickettsiaceae, and Pseudomonadaceae [24](https://doi.org/10.3390/ani14010097).

- **Co-infection Implications:** The presence of this bacterial consortium may enhance *P. salmonis* pathogenicity through cooperative interactions. When diagnosing skin ulcers, clinicians should consider that multiple pathogens may be present simultaneously, requiring molecular methods (multiplex PCR) to identify primary vs. opportunistic agents [24](https://doi.org/10.3390/ani14010097).  

### Treatment and Prevention

**Treatment Options**

- **Current Treatments:**
    - **Antibiotics:** Florfenicol is the primary antibiotic used, accounting for 87-97% of antimicrobial use for SRS in Chile. Oxytetracycline is also employed. In 2024, 351.1 tons of antibiotics were used in Chilean salmon aquaculture (9.7% increase from 2023), with over 96% directed at SRS control. The first half of 2024 showed a 22.6% increase compared to the same period in 2023, highlighting piscirickettsiosis as the major driver of antibiotic use in the Chilean salmon industry [7](https://www.fishfarmingexpert.com/antibiotics-chilean-salmon-farming-florfenicol/srs-pushed-chilean-salmon-farming-antibiotic-use-up-by-97-last-year/1968326) [8](https://doi.org/10.5772/intechopen.71780).
    - **PROA-Salmon Certification:** This voluntary program, managed by  Chile's National Fisheries Service (SERNAPESCA), recognizes farms that implement responsible antibiotic management. As of 2024, PROA-Salmon certified harvests represent 28.7% of production volume, with 34.5% of certified production being antibiotic-free in Chile. The 2019 [CSARP (Chilean Salmon Antibiotic Reduction Program)](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/globalassets/mba/pdf/conservation-and-science/csarp/monterey-bay-aquarium-csarp-plus-info-sheet-en.pdf) aimed for a 50% reduction by 2025. While it fell short of this goal, it spurred significant voluntary improvements. [5](https://www.aquafeed.com/newsroom/reports/chile-publishes-annual-reports-on-aquaculture-health-status-and-antimicrobial-use/) [31](https://www.seafoodsource.com/news/aquaculture/at-csarp-launch-chilean-salmon-industry-leaders-acknowledge-failure-to-develop-srs-vaccine-has-stymied-antibiotic-reduction-effort).
    - **Treatment Protocol:** Florfenicol at 20 mg/kg body weight for 15 days is the optimized dosing regimen [9](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0215174).
    - **In Vitro Sensitivity and resistance:** *P. salmonis* shows sensitivity to streptomycin, gentamicin, erythromycin, chloramphenicol, and oxytetracycline, but shows resistance to penicillin, penicillin G and spectinomycin in laboratory conditions [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia). Comparative genomics of 80 *P. salmonis* strains found antimicrobial resistance genes in four LF-genogroup strains, suggesting adaptation under selective pressure [27](https://doi.org/10.3390/ani15081176).

- **Treatment Challenges:** Because the bacterium partially survives and replicates inside the salmon's cells, antibiotic treatment often yields poor results [30](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/piscirickettsiose).
    - Medicated feed to control intracellular pathogens has proven largely unsuccessful, possibly due to difficulty achieving sufficient intracellular antibiotic concentrations [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia) [10](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aquaculture.2020.735323).
    - Treatment failure is common; farms with lower mortality when therapy is initiated have better outcomes [10](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aquaculture.2020.735323).

- **Vaccines:**
    - Approximately 34 commercial vaccines are licensed in Chile, with 94% injectable, 2% oral, and 1% immersion formulations [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.1019404).
    - The best-performing vaccine reduced mortality by only 22% compared to reference vaccination regimens [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.1019404).
    - Vaccine protection peaks at 600-800 degree-days post-vaccination and declines to pre-vaccination levels by 1800-1900 degree-days [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.1019404).

- **Why Vaccines Fail:**
    - *P. salmonis* employs sophisticated immune evasion mechanisms including intracellular survival within host cell vacuoles, cytokine manipulation (inhibiting IL-12 while inducing IL-10), and Type IV secretion system that inhibits phagosome-lysosome fusion [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.1019404).
    - Current vaccines do not activate the cellular-mediated immune responses necessary to control intracellular pathogens [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.1019404).
    - The protection is usually short-lived, where fish are often left unprotected during the final "fattening" stage at sea (3000+ degree-days) when they are most vulnerable to outbreaks [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.1019404).

**Preventive Measures**

- **Biosecurity Protocols:**
    - Early removal of mortalities and diseased fish to reduce infection pressure. Chile's General Sanitary Program for Mortality Management (PSGM) standardizes procedures for classification, handling, and safe disposal of fish mortalities [26](https://doi.org/10.1111/jfd.70097) [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia).
    - Broodstock screening and rejection of positive eggs [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.1019404) [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia).
    - Individual egg batch incubation to prevent cross-contamination [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.1019404) [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia).
    - Surveillance and genotyping of bacterial isolates are important for tracking circulating variants and supporting assessment of antimicrobial susceptibility for potential antibiotic use and control strategies [30](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/piscirickettsiose).

- **Farm Management Practices:**
    - Reduced stocking densities to decrease stress and transmission [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.1019404) [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia).
    - Site fallowing periods to break the infection cycle [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.1019404) [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia).
    - Control of sea lice coinfection which overrides vaccine protection. In Chilean farms (2014–2021), the level of egg-laying (ovigerous) female *Caligus* was a key predictor of weekly SRS mortality risk and of the first SRS outbreak of a production cycle, even though overall SRS mortality risk stayed constant while infestation levels rose [26](https://doi.org/10.1111/jfd.70097) [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.1019404).
    - Broodstock injection with antibiotics before seawater departure [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.1019404) [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia).
    - Knowledge gaps regarding reservoirs and transmission mechanisms have hindered the development of effective long-term control strategies [30](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/piscirickettsiose).

### Case Studies

**Real-World Examples**

- **Notable Outbreaks:**
    - **Chile (1989):** The first identified outbreak occurred in coho salmon, killing approximately 1.5 million fish and causing $10 million USD in economic losses. This was the first recognition of *P. salmonis* as a fish pathogen [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia) [3](https://www.int-res.com/journals/dao/articles/dao035107).
    - **Chile (1990):** The disease spread to Atlantic salmon with mortality rates reaching up to 90% on some farms [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia).
    - **Chile (2007-2009):** SRS contributed to a major aquaculture crisis alongside ISA outbreaks, devastating the Chilean salmon industry [4](https://doi.org/10.1128/mbio.03821-24).
    - **Chile (2024):** Environmental events including low oxygen levels and harmful algal blooms in Los Lagos and Aysén regions caused mortality peaks in January, with stable overall levels compared to previous years [5](https://www.aquafeed.com/newsroom/reports/chile-publishes-annual-reports-on-aquaculture-health-status-and-antimicrobial-use/).

- **Lessons Learned:** The importance of integrated disease management combining biosecurity, stress reduction, sea lice control, and early intervention. The limited efficacy of current vaccines highlights the need for continued research into cellular immune responses and novel vaccine approaches [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.1019404) [4](https://doi.org/10.1128/mbio.03821-24). 

## Data Insights

### Disease Impact by Country

#### Chile 

- **SRS Incidence in Chile:**
    - SRS is the most significant disease in Chilean salmon aquaculture and consistently accounts for the largest proportion of infectious disease-related mortality.
    - In 2020, SRS was responsible for 47.8% and 67.3% of mortality related to infectious diseases and 11.7% and 10.7% of total mortality in Atlantic salmon and rainbow trout production, respectively [4](https://doi.org/10.1128/mbio.03821-24).
    - Over recent years, SRS has remained the leading infectious cause of mortality, although its relative contribution has fluctuated. It accounted for 52.8% of infectious mortality in 2022, 44.7% in 2023, and 44% in the first half of 2024 [12](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2025/07/Informe-Situacion-Sanitaria-Salmonicultura-Ano-2024V-V2.pdf) [4](https://doi.org/10.1128/mbio.03821-24) [13](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2024/09/Informe-Sanitario-ANO-2023.pdf).
    - In the first half of 2024, 15.3 million salmonids died at marine sites. A harmful algal bloom (HAB) event in early January 2024 caused 2,854 tons of mortality [5](https://www.aquafeed.com/newsroom/reports/chile-publishes-annual-reports-on-aquaculture-health-status-and-antimicrobial-use/).
    - In the first half of 2025, SRS represented 60.1% of infectious mortality in Atlantic salmon [28](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2026/01/Informe-Situacion-Sanitaria-Salmonicultura-primer-semestre-2025-1.pdf) [12](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2025/07/Informe-Situacion-Sanitaria-Salmonicultura-Ano-2024V-V2.pdf).
    - Total marine site mortality remains substantial, with 13.6 million salmonids lost in the first half of 2025 and an average monthly seawater mortality of 0.91% (decreased from 1.15% in 1S 2024) [28](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2026/01/Informe-Situacion-Sanitaria-Salmonicultura-primer-semestre-2025-1.pdf).
    

- **Geographical Spread:**
    - The disease is prevalent throughout Chilean salmon farming regions. In 1S 2025: Los Lagos region reported 1.8% of production sites classified as *Centros de Alta Diseminación*- High-Dissemination Centers (CAD), while Aysén had 2% CAD (weekly average, increased from 0.7% in 1S 2024). A total of 22 CAD centers were registered in 1S 2025, and one confirmed site was recorded in the Magallanes region (ACS 46) [28](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2026/01/Informe-Situacion-Sanitaria-Salmonicultura-primer-semestre-2025-1.pdf).
    - In 1S 2024: Los Lagos had 12% of sites classified as alert centers and 1.8% CAD; Aysén had 10.9% alert centers and 0.7% CAD (weekly average). PSEVC-Piscirickettsiosis showed 73% of CAD centers in T3 stage (final production cycle) [28](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2026/01/Informe-Situacion-Sanitaria-Salmonicultura-primer-semestre-2025-1.pdf). No confirmed SRS sites were reported in the Magallanes region during 2024 [12](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2025/07/Informe-Situacion-Sanitaria-Salmonicultura-Ano-2024V-V2.pdf).

- **Economic Impact:**
    - Annual economic losses exceed $700 million USD, equivalent to approximately 10.5% of Chile's salmon exports (2022) [4](https://doi.org/10.1128/mbio.03821-24).
    - In 1989, losses were $10 million USD; by 1995, $49 million USD; and in some years, losses exceeded $100 million USD [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia).
    - Chile harvested 1,035,307 tons of salmon in 2024, making disease control critical to the industry [5](https://www.aquafeed.com/newsroom/reports/chile-publishes-annual-reports-on-aquaculture-health-status-and-antimicrobial-use/).

- **Treatment & Management:**
    - 231.19 tons of antibiotics were used in Chilean salmon aquaculture in the first half of 2025, a 6.34% increase compared to 1S 2024 (217.38 tons). 94.82% of antimicrobial use at closed cycle level was directed at Piscirickettsiosis treatment [29](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2026/01/Informe-de-uso-de-antimicrobianos-y-antiparasitarios-primer-semestre-2025.pdf).
    - The PROA-Salmón certification program has grown to cover 123,435 tons (27% of national production) in 1S 2025, promoting antimicrobial stewardship [29](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2026/01/Informe-de-uso-de-antimicrobianos-y-antiparasitarios-primer-semestre-2025.pdf).
    - 351.1 tons of antibiotics were used in Chilean salmon aquaculture in 2024, with over 96% directed at SRS treatment [5](https://www.aquafeed.com/newsroom/reports/chile-publishes-annual-reports-on-aquaculture-health-status-and-antimicrobial-use/) [11](https://doi.org/10.3390/antibiotics14121177) [14](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2025/07/Informe-Uso-Antimicrobianos-y-Antiparasitarios-Ano-2024.pdf).
    - Despite a downward trend in antibiotic use, there was a 3.6% increase in seawater use during the first half of 2024 due to environmental stressors [5](https://www.aquafeed.com/newsroom/reports/chile-publishes-annual-reports-on-aquaculture-health-status-and-antimicrobial-use/) [14](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2025/07/Informe-Uso-Antimicrobianos-y-Antiparasitarios-Ano-2024.pdf).
    - The First Congress on Sustainable Management of Aquaculture Bacterial Diseases was held in November 2024 in Puerto Varas, Chile, addressing SRS challenges [4](https://doi.org/10.1128/mbio.03821-24).

#### Norway

- **SRS Incidence in Norway:**
    - SRS has been reported in Norway since 1988, but with considerably lower incidence and economic impact than in Chile [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia)[30](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/piscirickettsiose).
    - The disease was detected at multiple locations in Norway in the late 1980s and again in 2002 [30](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/piscirickettsiose) [32](https://www.vetinst.no/rapporter-og-publikasjoner/rapporter/2025/norwegian-fish-health-report-2024). 
    - Over the past ~20 years, it has only been detected sporadically. However, several detections were reported in Nordland and Troms in 2024, although with limited reported mortality [30](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/piscirickettsiose) [32](https://www.vetinst.no/rapporter-og-publikasjoner/rapporter/2025/norwegian-fish-health-report-2024).
    - Genomic sequencing of Norwegian isolates indicates that they are genetically distinct and cluster separately from known Chilean isolates [32](https://www.vetinst.no/rapporter-og-publikasjoner/rapporter/2025/norwegian-fish-health-report-2024).
    - Sporadic outbreaks occur but do not reach the severity seen in Chilean aquaculture [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia) [30](https://www.vetinst.no/sykdom-og-agens/piscirickettsiose).


- **Treatment & Management:**
    - Norwegian farms employ similar biosecurity measures and treatment protocols as used globally [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia).

#### Canada

- **SRS Incidence in Canada:**
    - SRS has been reported in Canadian salmon farms, particularly in British Columbia [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia).
    - The disease impact is significantly lower than in Chile [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia).

#### Scotland & Ireland

- **SRS Incidence:**
    - Occasional outbreaks have been reported in both Scotland and Ireland [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia).
    - Incidence and losses are much lower than in Chile [1](https://thefishsite.com/disease-guide/salmonid-rickettsial-septicaemia).

## Research and References

### Latest Research Findings

Recent studies on SRS have focused on epidemiology, environmental reservoirs, genetic resistance, antimicrobial stewardship and pathogen genomics:

1. **"Epidemiology of Salmonid Rickettsial Septicaemia (SRS) in Farmed Salmon: The Role of Sea Lice Infestations in Mortality Risk"**
    Authors: Benjamín Diethelm-Varela, Nicolhole Atero, Francisca Córdova-Bührle, Enrico L. Rezende, Stefan Gelcich, Osvaldo Sandoval, Carlos Navarro, Fernando O. Mardones.
    Reference: Diethelm-Varela, B., Atero, N., Córdova-Bührle, F., Rezende, E. L., Gelcich, S., Sandoval, O., Navarro, C., & Mardones, F. O. (2026). Epidemiology of salmonid rickettsial septicemia (SRS) in farmed salmon: The role of sea lice infestations in mortality risk. *Journal of Fish Diseases*, *49*(5), e70097.
    Key findings: In a retrospective cohort of Chilean farms (2014–2021), SRS mortality risk was strongly seasonal and significantly higher in warmer seasons. *Caligus* infestation levels increased over the years while SRS mortality risk remained constant, but the level of egg-laying female sea lice was a key predictor of both weekly SRS mortality risk and the hazard of the first SRS outbreak of a production cycle.
    [Link to study](https://doi.org/10.1111/jfd.70097)

2. **"Free-living amoebae and their role in *Piscirickettsia salmonis* transmission in Chilean Salmon aquaculture: insights from *in vitro* and *in vivo* studies"**
    Authors: Fernando A. Gómez, Bruno Milesi, Sergio H. Marshall.
    Reference: Gómez, F. A., Milesi, B., & Marshall, S. H. (2026). Free-living amoebae and their role in *Piscirickettsia salmonis* transmission in Chilean salmon aquaculture: insights from *in vitro* and *in vivo* studies. *Frontiers in Microbiology*, *16*, 1711258.
    Key findings: *P. salmonis* was internalised, replicated and released by two free-living amoebae associated with salmon, *Acanthamoeba castellanii* and *Vannella* sp. *A. castellanii* maintained the bacteria stably, suggesting it can act as an environmental reservoir and vector that contributes to the persistence and spread of SRS.
    [Link to study](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2025.1711258)

3. **"A major QTL for resistance against Salmonid Rickettsial Septicaemia in coho salmon"** *(Preprint)*
    Authors: Thomas Moen, Fabian Grammes, Victor Martinez, Jacob Torgersen, Jørgen Ødegård, Tim Martin Knutsen, Tomasz Podgorniak, Robert Deerenberg, Daniela Cichero.
    Reference: Moen, T., Grammes, F., Martinez, V., Torgersen, J., Ødegård, J., Knutsen, T. M., Podgorniak, T., Deerenberg, R., & Cichero, D. (2025). *A major QTL for resistance against Salmonid Rickettsial Septicaemia in coho salmon* [Preprint]. bioRxiv.
    Key findings: Heritability of survival was 0.31 and 0.58 on the observed and liability scales. A QTL on chromosome 21 explained 26–97% of the genetic variation across 12 datasets, and the resistance allele was dominant. The most significant SNP lies in the first exon of the *hint3* gene and a long non-coding RNA gene, and was correlated with their expression.
    [Link to study](https://doi.org/10.64898/2025.12.08.689227)

4. **"Insights and Lessons from Chilean Salmon Aquaculture on Antimicrobial Use"**
    Authors: Daniela R. Farias, Rolando Ibarra, Felipe Tucca, Alexander Jaramillo-Torres, Javiera Cornejo, Pablo Ibieta, Fernando O. Mardones, Ruben Avendaño-Herrera.
    Reference: Farias, D. R., Ibarra, R., Tucca, F., Jaramillo-Torres, A., Cornejo, J., Ibieta, P., Mardones, F. O., & Avendaño-Herrera, R. (2025). Insights and lessons from Chilean salmon aquaculture on antimicrobial use. *Antibiotics*, *14*(12), 1177.
    Key findings: A workshop of 27 experts produced a roadmap for prudent antimicrobial use in Chilean salmon farming, where *P. salmonis* drove 351.1 tons of antimicrobial use in 2024. It covers antimicrobial resistance prevention under a One Health approach, gaps in veterinary training and communication, therapeutic alternatives given the limits of current vaccines, and missing data on environmental impact.
    [Link to study](https://doi.org/10.3390/antibiotics14121177)

5. **"Pathogenomic Insights into *Piscirickettsia salmonis* with a Focus on Virulence Factors, Single-Nucleotide Polymorphism Identification, and Resistance Dynamics"**
    Authors: Sk Injamamul Islam, Khandker Shahed, Md Imtiaz Ahamed, Luu Tang Phuc Khang, Won-Kyo Jung, Papungkorn Sangsawad, Nguyen Dinh-Hung, Patima Permpoonpattana, Nguyen Vu Linh.
    Reference: Islam, S. I., Shahed, K., Ahamed, M. I., Khang, L. T. P., Jung, W.-K., Sangsawad, P., Dinh-Hung, N., Permpoonpattana, P., & Linh, N. V. (2025). Pathogenomic insights into *Piscirickettsia salmonis* with a focus on virulence factors, single-nucleotide polymorphism identification, and resistance dynamics. *Animals*, *15*(8), 1176.
    Key findings: Comparison of 80 *P. salmonis* strains revealed an open pan-genome of 14,564 genes with 1,257 core genes. SNPs in *gyrA*, *dnaK*, *rpoB* and *ftsZ* distinguish the LF and EM genogroups, and antimicrobial resistance genes were found in four LF strains.
    [Link to study](https://doi.org/10.3390/ani15081176)

## Conclusion

Salmonid Rickettsial Septicaemia (SRS) remains the most significant threat to Chilean salmon aquaculture, with annual losses exceeding $700 million USD and driving the majority of antibiotic use in the industry. Despite 34 commercially available vaccines, their limited long-term efficacy (best showing only 22% mortality reduction) and the bacterium's sophisticated immune evasion mechanisms continue to pose major challenges.

Effective SRS management requires an integrated approach combining robust biosecurity protocols, early detection, stress reduction, sea lice control, and judicious antibiotic use. Ongoing research into cellular immune responses, novel vaccine approaches, genetic resistance and environmental reservoirs offers hope for improved control strategies. By implementing comprehensive disease management programs and supporting continued research, fish health managers and veterinarians can work toward reducing the devastating impact of SRS on salmon aquaculture operations.
