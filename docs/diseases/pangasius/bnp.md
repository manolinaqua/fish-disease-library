# Bacillary Necrosis of Pangasius (BNP)

## Species
Pangasianodon hypophthalmus

## Etiology
- Pathogen: *Edwardsiella ictaluri*
- Gram-negative bacterium

## Overview
Bacillary Necrosis of Pangasius (BNP) is a highly significant bacterial disease affecting Pangasius aquaculture, particularly in intensive farming regions of Southeast Asia.

## Clinical Signs
- White necrotic lesions in liver, kidney, spleen
- Lethargy and reduced feed intake
- Abnormal swimming behavior (surface swimming)
- Pale gills and weakened condition

## Epidemiology
- Common in high-density aquaculture systems
- Outbreaks often associated with:
  - temperature fluctuations
  - poor water quality
  - handling stress
- Peak prevalence during seasonal transitions

## Pathogenesis
The bacterium enters via gills or digestive tract, leading to systemic infection and internal organ necrosis.

## Diagnosis
- Gross pathology (internal white lesions)
- Bacterial culture
- PCR-based pathogen confirmation

## Treatment
- Antibiotic therapy (subject to regulations)
- Improved water quality management
- Supportive care

## Prevention
- Biosecurity practices
- Vaccination strategies (under development)
- Stress reduction (optimal stocking density)
- Environmental monitoring
