# Saprolegniasis (Fungal Infection)

## Species
Pangasianodon hypophthalmus

## Etiology
- Pathogen: *Saprolegnia* spp. (oomycete, fungus-like organism)

## Overview
Saprolegniasis is an opportunistic infection affecting Pangasius, typically occurring in fish that are stressed, injured, or exposed to poor environmental conditions.

## Clinical Signs
- Cotton-like white or gray patches on skin, fins, or gills
- Tissue erosion and ulceration
- Reduced activity and feeding
- Increased mortality in severe infections

## Epidemiology
- Common in freshwater aquaculture systems
- Strongly associated with:
  - physical injury
  - poor water quality
  - temperature fluctuations
- Often occurs as a secondary infection

## Pathogenesis
*Saprolegnia* colonizes damaged tissue, spreading rapidly across the surface and causing tissue necrosis and osmotic imbalance.

## Diagnosis
- Visual observation of characteristic cotton-like growth
- Microscopic confirmation of hyphae structure

## Treatment
- Antifungal treatments (e.g., formalin, potassium permanganate where permitted)
- Removal of infected individuals
- Improvement of environmental conditions

## Prevention
- Minimize handling stress and injury
- Maintain optimal water quality
- Regular monitoring of fish health
