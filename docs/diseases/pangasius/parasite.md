# Parasitic Infections in Pangasius

## Species
Pangasianodon hypophthalmus

## Etiology
- Protozoan parasites: *Trichodina spp.*, *Ichthyophthirius multifiliis*
- Myxozoan parasites: *Myxobolus spp.*, *Henneguya spp.*

## Overview
Parasitic infections are common in Pangasius aquaculture systems, particularly under high stocking densities and suboptimal water quality. These infections may cause chronic stress, reduced growth, and increased susceptibility to secondary infections.

## Clinical Signs
- Excess mucus production on skin and gills
- White spots (Ich infection)
- Gill damage and respiratory distress
- Erratic swimming and flashing behavior
- Reduced feeding

## Epidemiology
- Highly prevalent in intensive aquaculture systems
- Outbreaks associated with:
  - poor water quality
  - high organic load
  - overcrowding
- Transmission occurs via direct contact or contaminated water

## Pathogenesis
Parasites attach to skin or gills, causing mechanical damage and irritation, leading to impaired respiration and increased vulnerability to bacterial infections.

## Diagnosis
- Microscopic examination of skin and gill scrapings
- Observation of parasite morphology
- Behavioral symptoms

## Treatment
- Chemical treatments (formalin, salt baths, where permitted)
- Management of water quality
- Reduction of stocking density

## Prevention
- Regular monitoring of parasite load
- Biosecurity and quarantine protocols
- Proper pond hygiene and water management
